## 0. Constitutional directive

```text
PRESERVE APPROVED MEANING.
ADAPT EXECUTION TO TEMPORAL REALITY.

Focusa is rigid about canonical truth and flexible about execution.

Spec 140 compiles what the approved project instructions mean.
Spec 137 and Spec 137A govern time, deadlines, urgency, and temporal posture.
Spec 139 governs presence, placement, leases, deduplication, and execution admission.
Spec 144 governs semantic integrity, zero omission, and Build↔Verify truth.

Mission Canvas may reveal, explain, and control this system.
Mission Canvas does not create, own, or gate this system.
```

No model, agent, prompt, harness, client, Mission Canvas state, resource condition, deadline, release train, issue tracker, implementation phase, or degraded mode may weaken this directive.

## 1. Purpose

Spec 140 correctly establishes the Runtime Constitution as canonical and generated instruction artifacts as projections. This addendum closes the remaining high-consequence gaps:

1. preventing instruction integrity from becoming static procedural rigidity;
2. preventing temporal adaptation from becoming an undocumented scope-reduction mechanism;
3. preserving one temporal authority under Spec 137 rather than creating a second urgency engine in Spec 140;
4. separating operator steering, deadline revision, target-profile selection, and canonical amendment;
5. requiring two distinct operator acts before canonical instructions change;
6. making official documentation-sweep completion and confirmation part of amendment authority;
7. making instruction integrity foundational and headless rather than dependent on Mission Canvas or Agent Runtime Studio;
8. defining invariant versus temporally adaptive instruction dimensions;
9. preventing runtime invention of reduced or “deadline-safe” target states;
10. binding consequential operations to exact instruction, scope, target, deadline, presence, and environment revisions;
11. invalidating stale forecasts and plans after material canonical change;
12. preserving consistent behavior across agents, daemons, sessions, harnesses, and clients;
13. defining safe behavior when Mission Canvas, a harness surface, or Focusa dynamic authority is unavailable;
14. making instruction fidelity a verified admission and completion predicate.

This addendum is intentionally narrow. It does not create another project specification system, temporal authority, workflow engine, Mission Canvas authority, target-state authority, or generalized policy language.

- urgency and temporal posture;

Specs 135A, 135G, and 135I own Mission Canvas, Work Surfaces, generated UI, and typed operator projections.

Mission Canvas is a projection and command surface over canonical state. It is not an authority store, temporal engine, amendment engine, runtime guard owner, or completion authority.

## 3. Foundational laws

1. **Canonical meaning is preserved.** Approved requirements, primitives, boundaries, target states, and completion predicates remain authoritative until a confirmed canonical amendment becomes effective.
2. **Execution is temporally adaptive.** Instruction fidelity does not require one fixed procedure under every temporal posture.
3. **One temporal authority.** Spec 137 computes and owns deadline and urgency posture; Spec 140A cannot duplicate it.
4. **Mission Canvas independence.** Enabling, disabling, detaching, failing, or never installing Mission Canvas cannot change canonical behavior.
5. **Projection is not authority.** UI, prompt, AGENTS.md, skill, and harness artifacts explain or invoke canonical policy but do not own it.
6. **Steering is not amendment.** Current operator direction can prioritize and select authorized routes but does not silently change canon.
7. **Deadline revision is not amendment.** Changing an operator deadline through Spec 137 does not by itself alter project meaning or required outcomes.
8. **Target selection is not target invention.** Spec 137 may select an approved target profile; no runtime actor may create a reduced target because time is short.
9. **Only confirmed amendments change canon.** Detailed authorization alone is insufficient; the completed official documentation sweep must be explicitly confirmed.
10. **The prior canon remains active until confirmation.** Proposed or authorized-but-unconfirmed language cannot govern canonical implementation or completion.
11. **Urgency narrows execution, not truth.** Rising pressure reduces speculation and unrelated work while preserving mandatory authority, safety, evidence, and settlement truth.
12. **Ambiguity does not authorize improvisation.** The affected slice blocks or escalates while other compliant unblocked work continues.
13. **Convenience cannot redefine equivalence.** A substitute is equivalent only when the governing contract permits equivalence and proof establishes equal-or-stronger semantics.
14. **No hidden scope shrinkage.** “Deadline-safe,” “minimal,” “degraded,” “partial,” or equivalent labels cannot erase remaining obligations or inflate completion claims.
15. **Dynamic posture does not mutate the stable prompt.** Ordinary urgency changes use fresh runtime guards outside the session-stable constitutional prefix.
16. **Material canon change requires governed rebind.** Affected sessions cannot silently continue consequential work under stale stable instructions.
17. **Consequential work is revision-bound.** Mutation, side effect, validation, and settlement bind to exact instruction, target, scope, deadline, environment, and presence references.
18. **Stale authority fails closed.** Missing or stale dynamic guards cannot be converted into permission or completion.
19. **Hard guarantees are runtime-native.** Prompt adherence alone cannot satisfy a mandatory instruction-integrity control.
20. **Every surface is optional; canonical behavior is not.** API, CLI, Pi, TUI, Mission Canvas, menubar, and future clients must project one core behavior.
21. **Blocked is visible and blocking.** A blocked amendment, conflict, target, or guard remains open and cannot be narrated away.
22. **Instruction fidelity is a completion predicate.** Work cannot settle when required meaning, surfaces, routes, or proof were weakened or omitted.
23. **Corrections append.** Instruction, amendment, guard, and adaptation records are revisioned and superseded; history is not rewritten in place.
24. **No full Spec 140 conformance without Spec 140A.** Parent-only implementation cannot claim complete Runtime Constitution integrity.

### 4.1 Invariant classes

The following classes cannot be weakened by temporal posture:

- `semantic_invariant` — required externally or internally meaningful behavior;
- `authority_invariant` — permission, approval, operator, reducer, and closure boundaries;
- `safety_privacy_invariant` — safety, security, privacy, retention, and destructive-action boundaries;
- `completion_invariant` — target-state, Evidence, Receipt, reconciliation, and settlement predicates;
- `execution_placement_invariant` — placement rules unless an already-approved alternate venue or route applies.

### 4.2 Temporally adaptive class

A `temporally_adaptive` claim permits Spec 137 to adjust only its recorded dimensions. Examples include:

- planning depth;
- exploratory research budget;
- number of alternatives compared;
- implementation batch size;
- checkpoint frequency;
- coordination and status cadence;
- eligible concurrency;
- verification scheduling or reuse;
- escalation threshold;
- unrelated optional polish;
- dependency-ready sequence;
- use of an already-approved contingency route.

### 4.3 Operator-selectable class

An `operator_selectable` claim presents multiple canonical choices whose meanings are already approved. Selecting one does not amend canon.

### 4.4 Implementation discretion

`implementation_discretion` applies only where the governing requirement leaves the dimension open and the choice does not change observable behavior, primitive ownership, required surfaces, authority, temporal truth, compatibility, migration, Evidence, or closure.

Unclassified material claims are not presumed adaptive. They remain unresolved and block the affected consequential slice until classified or confirmed non-material.

The envelope is stable and versioned. Spec 137 supplies the changing temporal decision.

### 5.1 Permitted temporal adaptations

When allowed by the envelope and other governing authorities, Spec 137 MAY cause the runtime to:

- reorder dependency-ready work;
- reduce speculative research;
- choose a smaller verified implementation batch;
- increase checkpoint and Evidence cadence;
- increase or reduce concurrency within capability and lease limits;
- freeze unrelated optional work;
- reuse an equivalent valid active build, test, or verification run;
- escalate blockers sooner;
- shorten explanatory communication while preserving truth;
- select a pre-approved execution route or target profile whose activation conditions are satisfied;
- prioritize the smallest safe route to delivery, reconciliation, proof, and settlement after breach.

### 5.2 Forbidden temporal adaptations

Temporal posture MUST NOT:

- change canonical requirement meaning;
- grant permission, capability, approval, lease, or closure authority;
- remove an accepted requirement from the closure graph;
- fabricate non-applicability;
- invent a target state;
- rename partial completion as full completion;
- waive mandatory verification;
- convert unavailable proof into a pass;
- silently switch to an unauthorized venue, model, verifier, or route;
- weaken security, privacy, accessibility, compatibility, migration, Evidence, or Receipt obligations;
- silently edit official documentation;
- treat a prompt or UI state as sufficient enforcement.

## 7. Operator action taxonomy

### 7.1 Execution steering

Execution steering may:

- prioritize or sequence work;
- narrow the immediate task;
- pause or resume work;
- select among approved routes or targets;
- direct critical-path focus;
- request investigation, evidence, or an amendment proposal;
- stop consequential action.

Execution steering does not change canonical documentation.

### 7.2 Temporal revision

A deadline or readiness-target change is owned by Spec 137 and requires its authenticated, authorized, audited reducer path.

A temporal revision MUST preserve the prior revision, recompute affected temporal state, and reassess active Workpoints and forecasts. It does not require a documentation sweep unless it also changes project meaning, target state, or required behavior.

### 7.3 Canonical amendment

A canonical amendment changes or materially reinterprets approved requirement meaning, target state, primitive ownership, required surfaces, authority boundaries, validation, proof, migration, compatibility, or closure.

Only the canonical amendment path may make such a change effective.

Canonical change requires two distinct operator acts:

1. **Detailed amendment authorization** after review of the exact proposed semantic and operational impact.
2. **Post-sweep canonical confirmation** after all official documents and machine-readable artifacts have been updated, reconciled, regenerated, diffed, and presented.

Authorization to perform the documentation sweep is not confirmation that the resulting sweep is canonical.

### 8.4 Effectivity rule

A canonical amendment becomes effective only when:

- the exact amendment has detailed operator authorization;
- the official documentation sweep is complete;
- every affected normative source and machine-readable contract is updated;
- unresolved contradictions are cleared or explicitly remain blocking;
- the complete post-sweep diff is reviewed;
- the operator explicitly confirms the completed sweep as canonical;
- a new immutable Runtime Constitution revision is approved and activated;
- required Receipts are committed.

Until then, the previous approved Runtime Constitution and official documentation remain canonical.

### 8.5 No edit-while-deciding

Agents MAY draft proposals and sweep patches for review. They MUST NOT treat proposed text, an in-progress sweep, an unconfirmed branch, or an authorized idea as effective project instruction.

The affected canonical implementation slice remains blocked from adopting the proposed semantics while other compliant, unblocked work continues.

## 11. Temporal, canonical, and session interlocks

### 11.1 Urgency change

An ordinary Spec 137 urgency or temporal-posture change:

- does not change the Runtime Constitution revision;
- does not require stable-prompt recompilation;
- refreshes the temporal guard and selected adaptation;
- may change sequence, route, cadence, or eligible work inside the active envelope;
- preserves current canonical meaning and target state.

### 11.2 Deadline revision

An authorized deadline or readiness-target revision:

- creates a new temporal revision;
- preserves the prior deadline record;
- invalidates or reassesses affected estimates;
- recomputes urgency, slack, safety margin, and critical path;
- reassesses active Workpoints and selected adaptations;
- does not require a canonical amendment unless project meaning or target state changes.

### 11.3 Canonical amendment activation

When a canonical amendment becomes effective:

- a new immutable Runtime Constitution revision is created;
- affected plans, estimates, validation assumptions, and target bindings are marked stale until reassessed;
- affected sessions safely stop at the next governed boundary or complete a currently non-reversible atomic action;
- affected sessions rebind, fork, or restart before further consequential work;
- pending operations compare their bound revisions and fail or re-evaluate when stale;
- Spec 137 recomputes temporal consequences;
- Spec 139 reassesses execution admission where placement or topology implications changed;
- Spec 144 reassesses semantic and verification impact;
- Evidence and Receipts preserve the transition.

### 11.4 Forecast invalidation

A material instruction, scope, target, dependency, acceptance, validation, migration, or proof change MUST trigger forecast and critical-path impact assessment. An old estimate cannot remain authoritative merely because its original expiry has not elapsed.

### 11.5 Compaction and model changes

Compaction, model switching, provider overflow, and transcript loss reload:

- the pinned stable Runtime Constitution identity;
- the current instruction-integrity references;
- fresh Spec 137 and Spec 139 dynamic guards;
- the current Workpoint and target-state binding.

Transcript memory does not supersede these records.

## 13. Conflict, ambiguity, and feasibility behavior

When a material instruction is ambiguous, contradictory, missing, infeasible, unsafe, or incompatible with the current environment, the agent and runtime MUST:

1. identify exact source clauses, revisions, and requirement IDs;
2. identify the affected paths, operations, targets, surfaces, and completion claims;
3. preserve the strongest currently approved interpretation that does not invent behavior;
4. block mutation only for the affected consequential slice unless broader safety or semantic impact requires more;
5. calculate temporal, critical-path, dependency, proof, and closure impact;
6. record bounded alternatives as proposals rather than effective instructions;
7. request the required operator decision or amendment authority;
8. continue the highest-priority compliant and unblocked work;
9. preserve the blocker in the closure graph;
10. refuse to split the difference or silently choose the easier interpretation.

A feasibility finding may justify an amendment proposal. It does not itself authorize deviation.

## 14. Degraded and unavailable posture

### 14.1 Mission Canvas unavailable

Mission Canvas unavailability is a presentation degradation only. Canonical operations continue through supported headless and alternate client paths.

### 14.2 Harness capability unavailable

When a harness cannot load, inspect, or enforce a rule:

- the target capability gap is explicit;
- daemon, Operation Registry, permission, hook, shell, CI, or branch controls provide backstops where supported;
- the system does not claim target enforcement that did not occur;
- a different supported client or headless route remains available for canonical operations.

### 14.3 Focusa dynamic authority unavailable

When fresh temporal, environment, presence, or admission authority cannot be obtained:

- the last verified stable Runtime Constitution may support bounded inspection, drafting, and non-consequential planning;
- dynamic state is reported as unknown or stale;
- consequential operations requiring fresh authority block or escalate;
- stale full authority cannot masquerade as current authority;
- completion and settlement requiring fresh proof cannot proceed.

### 14.4 Recovery

Recovery MUST preserve prior guards, amendment state, target selection, open conflicts, temporal incidents, Evidence, and Receipts. Restart cannot reset an unresolved integrity failure.

## 17. Verification and conformance

### 17.1 Instruction fidelity verification

Verification MUST compare implementation and effective runtime behavior against:

- active governing requirement IDs;
- primitive ownership;
- invariant and adaptive classifications;
- active target profile;
- permitted route and adaptation;
- required surfaces;
- validation and proof obligations;
- active amendment and Runtime Constitution revisions;
- temporal, environment, and presence guards;
- remaining closure obligations.

Verification fails when:

- required semantics were weakened;
- a specified primitive was replaced without approved equivalence;
- an applicable surface or route was omitted;
- an unauthorized target or temporal adaptation was used;
- implementation adopted an amendment before post-sweep confirmation;
- documentation was rewritten after implementation to manufacture compliance;
- stale instruction, scope, target, deadline, environment, or presence authority was used;
- Mission Canvas state changed canonical behavior;
- unavailable proof was converted into a pass;
- required Evidence, Receipts, or closure mappings are absent.

### 17.2 Mandatory scenario matrix

At minimum, tests MUST cover:

1. Mission Canvas enabled.
2. Mission Canvas disabled.
3. Mission Canvas unavailable after session start.
4. Mission Canvas never installed.
5. CLI-only operation.
6. Pi-only operation with no Mission Canvas projection.
7. API-driven headless operation.
8. Ordinary urgency change without prompt restart.
9. Deadline revision with forecast and critical-path reassessment.
10. Critical posture that narrows work without changing target meaning.
11. Breached recovery selecting the smallest approved safe route.
12. Attempted runtime invention of a reduced target.
13. Operator steering that does not amend canon.
14. Detailed amendment authorization without completed sweep.
15. Completed sweep without operator post-sweep confirmation.
16. Effective amendment only after both operator acts.
17. Canonical amendment invalidating stale estimates and plans.
18. Session rebind after material amendment.
19. Multi-agent stale-revision rejection.
20. Harness lacking enforcement with daemon backstop.
21. Dynamic authority outage with bounded safe fallback.
22. Compaction recovery preserving stable constitution and refreshing dynamic guards.
23. Unresolved ambiguity blocking only the affected slice.
24. Instruction-fidelity failure preventing completion and settlement.

### 17.3 Mission Canvas parity proof

For every canonical operation, tests MUST prove equivalent authority and state transitions with Mission Canvas enabled and disabled. UI-specific rendering is excluded from behavioral parity; canonical reads, writes, approvals, guards, and verification are not.

### 17.4 No prompt-only proof

A successful model response, generated AGENTS.md, loaded system prompt, or visible Mission Canvas card does not prove runtime enforcement. Conformance requires core state, reducer events, daemon behavior, generated contracts, operation admission, tests, Evidence, and Receipts.

## 19. Acceptance criteria

Spec 140A is accepted only when:

1. Spec 140 and Spec 140A are treated as one combined normative instruction-integrity source.
2. Instruction meaning remains strict while execution adapts through Spec 137-owned temporal posture.
3. Spec 140A does not compute or duplicate urgency, deadlines, or critical-path authority.
4. Every material instruction claim has a governed adaptability classification.
5. Spec 137 can select only adaptations present in an approved envelope.
6. Runtime actors cannot invent reduced targets or erase remaining obligations.
7. Operator steering, deadline revision, approved target selection, and canonical amendment are distinct operations.
8. Canonical amendment requires detailed operator authorization and separate post-sweep canonical confirmation.
9. The prior canon remains effective until the completed sweep is confirmed and a new immutable Runtime Constitution revision is activated.
10. Amendment activation invalidates affected forecasts, plans, target bindings, and validation assumptions.
11. Ordinary urgency changes do not require stable-prompt mutation or session restart.
12. Material canonical changes require affected sessions to rebind, fork, or restart before consequential continuation.
13. InstructionIntegrityGuard is reducer/daemon-owned and checked at consequential boundaries.
14. Consequential operations bind exact constitution, instruction, target, scope, deadline, environment, presence, and Workpoint revisions.
15. Mission Canvas enabled, disabled, unavailable, and absent states produce equivalent canonical behavior.
16. API, CLI, and Pi provide supported headless inspection, mutation, approval, guard, and verification routes.
17. UI and prompt surfaces remain projections and cannot independently grant authority or completion.
18. Multi-agent and multi-daemon stale instruction states fail or re-evaluate safely.
19. Dynamic authority outage produces bounded unknown posture rather than stale full authority.
20. Ambiguity and feasibility findings block only affected slices when broader integrity does not require a wider stop.
21. Instruction fidelity is verified against active requirements, routes, surfaces, adaptation, target, guards, and proof.
22. Unauthorized deviation, premature amendment adoption, or Mission Canvas-dependent behavior blocks completion.
23. Evidence and Receipts prove classification, envelope approval, amendment authorization, sweep completion, confirmation, activation, guard admission, rebind, verification, and rollback.
24. Full Spec 140 conformance is impossible while any applicable Spec 140A requirement is missing, partial, blocked, disputed, or implemented-unverified.
